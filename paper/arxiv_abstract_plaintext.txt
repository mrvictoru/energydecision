Grid-scale batteries in Australia's National Electricity Market must co-optimise energy arbitrage with eight frequency-control ancillary service (FCAS) markets while paying for battery wear. We explore whether an offline sequence model - a Decision Transformer - can act as a single, generalising controller for this multi-market dispatch problem: learning from logged data rather than risky online exploration, and optimising revenue net of degradation. We first train it on trajectories generated by online reinforcement-learning policies (PPO and other actor-critic agents). That route stalls at a behaviour-cloning ceiling: the logged policies bid little into FCAS, and none of the tested changes to data scale or curation, return-to-go prompting, loss re-weighting, or online fine-tuning lets the Decision Transformer exceed the FCAS behaviour present in its logs. We therefore change the data source rather than the learner, distilling an honest, non-clairvoyant stochastic-dynamic-programming planning teacher into a standalone Decision Transformer and conditioning it on a state-dependent return-to-go prompt derived from the planner's cost-to-go. The resulting policy beats a tuned PPO baseline on four held-out surfaces (standard, dispatch-matched, expanded broad-2024, and 2025 out-of-distribution) by 3.84x, 1.80x, 6.70x, and 6.39x, with all four paired-difference 95% confidence intervals excluding zero, and passes a market-impact gate on all nine grid-scale cells with no solver in the control loop. Results are simulator-based; sim-to-real transfer is the main open question.
