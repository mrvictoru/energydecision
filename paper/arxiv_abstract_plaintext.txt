Grid-scale batteries in Australia's National Electricity Market must co-optimise energy arbitrage with eight frequency-control ancillary service (FCAS) markets while paying for wear. We ask whether an offline Decision Transformer can control this multi-market problem from a fixed corpus. A first route, using trajectories from online RL policies, stalls at a behaviour-cloning ceiling: the logged policies bid little into FCAS, and tested changes to data scale, prompting, losses, architecture and fine-tuning do not close the gap. We instead distil an honest, non-clairvoyant dynamic-programming planner into a standalone Decision Transformer conditioned on a state-dependent planner cost-to-go prompt. Against three seeded, validation-tuned PPO checkpoints trained for 4M environment steps, the DT earns 1.70x, 1.20x, 1.69x and 1.60x the per-scenario seed-mean PPO profit on four held-out surfaces. The paired 95% interval excludes zero on three surfaces but includes zero on dispatch-matched; individual PPO seeds change the ordering on three surfaces. Under a piecewise market-impact stress model, the seed-mean ratio is 1.60x (8/9 wins), while seed-specific ratios span 0.91-2.96x. No per-decision optimisation runs in the control loop, but a planner value table is precomputed. Results are simulator-based; sim-to-real transfer remains open.
