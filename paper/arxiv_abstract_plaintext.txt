Grid-scale batteries in Australia's National Electricity Market must co-optimise energy arbitrage with eight frequency-control ancillary service (FCAS) markets while paying for wear. We explore whether an offline Decision Transformer can act as a single, generalising controller for multi-market dispatch: learning from a fixed offline corpus rather than risky online exploration, and evaluated on profit net of degradation. We first train it on trajectories from online reinforcement-learning policies (proximal policy optimisation (PPO) and other actor-critic agents). That route stalls at a behaviour-cloning ceiling: the logged policies bid little into FCAS, and none of the tested changes to data scale, return-to-go prompting, loss re-weighting, action heads, architecture, or fine-tuning closes the FCAS-bidding gap to PPO. We therefore change the data source rather than the learner, distilling a non-clairvoyant dynamic-programming planner into a standalone Decision Transformer and conditioning it on a state-dependent return-to-go prompt from the planner's cost-to-go. Against three seeded, validation-tuned PPO checkpoints trained for 4M environment steps, the DT earns 1.70x, 1.20x, 1.69x, and 1.60x the per-scenario seed-mean PPO profit on four held-out surfaces (standard, dispatch-matched, expanded broad-2024, and 2025 out-of-distribution). The paired 95% interval excludes zero on three surfaces but includes zero on dispatch-matched; individual PPO seeds change the ordering on three surfaces. Under a piecewise market-impact stress model, the DT retains a seed-mean ratio of 1.60x and wins 8/9 cells, while the ratio across PPO seeds spans 0.91-2.96x. No per-decision optimization runs in the control loop, but a precomputed planner value table is required. Results are simulator-based; sim-to-real transfer is the main open question.
