\subsection{SDP}
% NOTE(author): \citet{abdulla2016optimal} is a THIRD-PARTY paper (de Hoog et al.),

Stochastic dynamic programming (SDP) is a finite-horizon planning method that solves sequential decision problems by backward induction: at each state, it selects the action that minimizes immediate cost plus the cost-to-go under a forecast model. In our setting, the state is the battery state of charge and time, the action is charge/discharge, and the objective combines energy cost/revenue with degradation and constraint handling. We do not re-derive the full SDP formulation here; instead, we refer readers to \citet{abdulla2016optimal} for a detailed degradation-aware storage-planning treatment and focus on the specific non-clairvoyant teacher used in this work.
\subsection{SDP Planner/Teacher}
The teacher uses a hybrid, non-clairvoyant control pipeline. For energy
dispatch, it constructs a Regional Reference Price (RRP) forecast by averaging
historical RRP within month-of-year and hour-of-day bins. The profile is estimated from pre-2024 processed data and mapped to the settlement times
in each episode. Thus, the energy planner receives a seasonal estimate of
future RRP rather than the episode's realized future prices. Frequency Control Ancillary Services (FCAS) is treated separately: the bidding rule uses
the prices available at the current step, without forecasting future FCAS prices.
We use ``honest'' to distinguish this information set from a perfect-foresight 
planner given realized future prices.

The control pipeline has three stages. First, a learned State of Charge (SOC) waypoint
model proposes a sequence of target states of charge; it is a helper that
sets intermediate targets, not an optimizer. Second, for each interval
between waypoints, a discretized dynamic program performs backward
induction over SOC and candidate charge/discharge actions. It minimizes
forecast energy cost plus an estimated degradation cost and a soft
terminal penalty for deviation from the segment's target SOC. The wear
estimate combines the planner's degradation estimator with a linear
throughput surrogate, scaled by the calibration parameter
\texttt{deg\_calibration}. Third, after the energy action is selected, a
greedy bidder assigns available raise and lower headroom to the
highest-priced eligible FCAS service in each direction, using current
FCAS prices. Consequently, this is a hybrid energy-planning and
rule-based FCAS pipeline, not a pure SDP solver or a joint optimizer
over energy and FCAS.

Separately, we compute a free-terminal cost-to-go table
$J_t(s)$ over time $t$ and discretized battery SOC $s$. Its backward
recursion estimates the remaining forecast energy and degradation cost
from each time--SOC pair, with zero terminal cost by default; it is not
conditioned on reaching a waypoint. At inference, the table is looked up
at the current time and SOC, and the Decision Transformer receives
$-J_t(s)$ as its return-to-go prompt. This converts estimated remaining
cost to a return-valued conditioning signal; it does not give the student
the SDP policy or run the SDP optimizer at inference.
